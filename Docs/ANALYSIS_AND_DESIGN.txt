1. PROBLEM ANALYSIS
======================================================================

The university still keeps its academic records by hand. Because of
this, it is hard to manage student records, organize courses, assign
lecturers, and track registrations. The system I am building stores
departments, students, student profiles, lecturers, courses, and
course registrations.

I read the problem statement line by line and wrote down the rules it
gives me:

R-a. The university has several departments.
R-b. A department offers many courses. A course belongs to one department.
R-c. A department employs many lecturers.
R-d. Every department has one lecturer who serves as its head.
R-e. A student is admitted into one department.
R-f. A student has exactly one profile. A profile belongs to one student.
R-g. A lecturer teaches many courses. A course has many lecturers.
     The assignment says this must not get a separate entity.
R-h. A student registers for many courses. A course has many students.
     Each registration records the date, academic year, semester,
     status (PENDING, APPROVED, or REJECTED), and grade.

======================================================================
2. TASK 1 - ENTITIES
======================================================================

I found six entities. I looked for the things in the statement that
have details of their own, and for links between things that carry
data of their own.

1. Department
   The university has several of them. A department owns courses,
   lecturers, and students, so it sits at the centre of the model.

2. Student
   A person admitted into the university and placed in one department.

3. StudentProfile
   The statement says each student has one personal profile with a
   date of birth, gender, phone number, and address. These details
   belong together, so I made the profile its own entity with a
   one-to-one link to Student.

4. Lecturer
   A person employed by a department. One lecturer also serves as
   head of that department.

5. Course
   A subject offered by one department.

6. Registration
   The link between a student and a course carries its own data:
   date, academic year, semester, status, and grade. A link that
   carries data needs to be an entity. This is my join entity.

I did not create an entity for the Lecturer and Course link. The
statement says it must be kept without a separate entity, so a plain
join table handles it.

4. TASKS 3 AND 4 - RELATIONSHIPS AND CARDINALITY
======================================================================

No.  Relationship                      Cardinality   Implemented with
---  --------------------------------  ------------  -----------------------------
1    Department offers Course          One to many   courses.department_id (FK)
2    Department employs Lecturer       One to many   lecturers.department_id (FK)
3    Department has head Lecturer      One to one    departments.head_lecturer_id (FK, unique)
4    Department admits Student         One to many   students.department_id (FK)
5    Student has StudentProfile        One to one    student_profiles.student_id (FK, unique)
6    Lecturer teaches Course           Many to many  lecturer_courses (join table)
7    Student registers for Course      Many to many  registrations (join entity)
     Student makes Registration        One to many   registrations.student_id (FK)
     Course receives Registration      One to many   registrations.course_id (FK)

My reasoning for each one

1. A department offers several courses, and each course belongs to
   one department.
2. A department employs several lecturers, and each lecturer works in
   one department.
3. Each department has one head, and a lecturer heads at most one
   department. I kept this apart from relationship 2. Relationship 2
   says where a lecturer works. Relationship 3 says who leads the
   department. The head column allows an empty value in the database.
   This lets me save a department first, then its lecturers, then set
   the head. The rule that the head must work in the same department
   belongs in the service layer, which comes in a later stage.
4. Each student is assigned to one department, and a department has
   many students.
5. A student has one profile and a profile belongs to one student.
6. A lecturer teaches many courses and a course has many lecturers.
   No separate entity is allowed, so I used a join table.
7. A student registers for many courses and a course has many
   students. The link carries data, so it becomes the Registration
   entity. This splits the many-to-many into two one-to-many links.

5. TASK 5 - RELATIONSHIPS THAT NEED A FOREIGN KEY
======================================================================

In every one-to-many link I put the foreign key on the many side.
In the one-to-one links I put it on the table that depends on the
other one.

1. courses.department_id        references departments.id
2. lecturers.department_id      references departments.id
3. departments.head_lecturer_id references lecturers.id (unique)
4. students.department_id       references departments.id
5. student_profiles.student_id  references students.id (unique)
7. registrations.student_id     references students.id
   registrations.course_id      references courses.id

A profile has no meaning without its student, so its foreign key sits in
student_profiles. For the head, I placed the column in departments.
The lecturers table already holds the department link from
relationship 2, and a second department column there would be
confusing.

======================================================================
6. TASK 6 - RELATIONSHIP THAT NEEDS A JOIN TABLE
======================================================================

Lecturer to Course (many to many, relationship 6).

Table: lecturer_courses
  lecturer_id  FK to lecturers.id
  course_id    FK to courses.id
  Primary key: (lecturer_id, course_id)

The table holds no other column. That is why it needs no entity.

======================================================================
7. TASK 7 - RELATIONSHIP THAT NEEDS A JOIN ENTITY
======================================================================

Student to Course (many to many, relationship 7).

Entity: Registration. The link holds five extra attributes: the
registration date, academic year, semester, status, and grade. A plain
join table has no place for extra columns, so I used an entity.

Registration has its own generated id and two foreign keys, one to
Student and one to Course. I added a unique constraint on
(student_id, course_id, academic_year, semester). It stops a student
from registering for the same course twice in the same term. A student
still retakes a course in a later term.

departments (

  head_lecturer_id FK -> lecturers.id

lecturers (

  last_name,

  code,

  department_id FK -> departments.id

  last_name,
  email,
  department_id FK -> departments.id

  id PK,
  date_of_birth,
  gender,
  phone_number,
  address,
  student_id FK -> students.id (unique)

  id PK,
  student_id FK -> students.id,

  academic_year,
  semester,
  status,
  grade
)
